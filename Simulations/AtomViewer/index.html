How this works

Every orbital is computed, not drawn from a picture. Each atom is solved as a self-consistent field: guess the electrons' potential, solve the Schrödinger equation for every occupied shell in it, rebuild the potential from where those electrons then sit, and repeat until nothing changes. This is density-functional theory in the local density approximation, and it matches NIST's reference calculations to about a millionth of a hartree.

An atom is round. Filled subshells add up to a sphere, so “All electrons” is a fuzzy ball with shells inside it (try Cutaway with Shells). The famous lobes appear when you pick one subshell or one orbital out of it, from the chips above the view or the boxes below.

The nucleus is tens of thousands of times smaller than the atom, which is why it gets a view of its own. Whether it holds together is looked up in the IAEA chart of every nucleus ever observed. Anything not on the chart has never been made, and a liquid-drop model then says whether it could exist.

What is left out: relativity (gold’s 6s shell comes out roughly a fifth too large here, which is also why this gold would not look gold), spin–orbit splitting, and anything molecular. Molecules are next.
