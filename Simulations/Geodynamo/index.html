A planet makes its own magnetic field — and now and then loses track of which way is north.

Core conditions

Earth today: vigorous convection, rapid rotation, a whisper of differential rotation, and turbulence enough to knock the dipole over every few hundred thousand years.

Convective vigour—

Heat escaping the core stirs the liquid iron. No stirring, no dynamo.

Rotation rate—

Coriolis gives the convection a handedness. Without it the twists cancel and the field never adds up.

Differential rotation—

Winds the poloidal field into a toroidal one. At zero the dipole locks; wind it up and the field becomes a metronome.

Turbulence—

The convection is not smooth. These fluctuations are what actually trigger a reversal.
